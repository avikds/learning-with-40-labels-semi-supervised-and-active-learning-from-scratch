Learning with 40 Labels: Semi-Supervised and Active Learning from Scratch

How it was built

Part 1: Forty Labels and a Baseline

The problem and the tools every later method reuses: a seeded MNIST split with four labels per digit, a 10,000-image unlabeled pool and a fixed test set; a ReLU MLP in tinygrad; a hand-written Adam; batched prediction and accuracy; label-preserving augmentation as vicinal risk minimization (section 19.1); and one TinyJit training loop that adds any unlabeled loss with a ramped weight.

Part 2: Label Propagation on a Graph

Section 19.3.4: a PCA embedding fit on the unlabeled pool, a symmetric kNN similarity graph with Gaussian weights, the book's label-propagation iteration with the transition matrix of eq 19.26, its closed-form harmonic fixed point, and a soft-target trainer that turns the propagated labels (a transductive result) into an MLP that classifies new images (an inductive one).

Part 3: Self-Training and Entropy

Sections 19.3.1-19.3.2: pseudo-labels selected by a confidence threshold with diagnostics against the pool's hidden labels; offline self-training rounds that expose confirmation bias; the online self-training, entropy-minimization and sharpened losses of Fig 19.9; and the input-output mutual information objective of eq 19.25, whose marginal-entropy term keeps predictions on the pool from collapsing.

Part 4: Consistency and Virtual Adversarial Training

Section 19.3.5: the squared-error and KL consistency losses of eq 19.27 and Fig 19.11, the virtual adversarial direction of eq 19.28 found by one gradient step from a random direction (and why its xi must respect float32 resolution), and the VAT loss of eq 19.29 with the mutual-information term as an option.

Part 5: Self-Supervised Pretraining and Distillation

Sections 19.2.4.4 and 19.3.7: the SimCLR contrastive loss of eq 19.7 (approximate maximum likelihood of a conditional energy-based model, eq 19.8-19.10), contrastive pretraining of an MLP encoder on the unlabeled pool, swapping its projection head for a classifier head to fine-tune on 40 labels, and distilling a teacher's tempered predictions on the pool into a smaller student (eq 19.45).

Part 6: Active Learning

Section 19.4: a five-member ensemble trained in one batched TinyJit step as a cheap stand-in for posterior samples, the max-entropy, least-confident and BALD acquisition scores (eq 19.51), and a pool-based active-learning loop that grows 20 labels to 120 in batches of 20 and records the learning curve for each acquisition rule.

Part 7: The Report

One function that runs the whole study on one label draw: supervised baselines with and without augmentation, label propagation (iterative, closed form and distilled), offline self-training rounds, entropy minimization, mutual information, VAT with and without the MI term, SimCLR pretraining with two fine-tuning recipes, distillation into a small student, and the three active-learning curves, returned as a dict with timings for the completed project to print.
